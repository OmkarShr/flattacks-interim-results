Three takeaways

  1. 01
    DAGER is strongest on singleton victims; scores fall sharply in the larger tested cells.

    It leads both M=1 cells (RL 0.314 and 0.339), while RL is 0.042–0.061 in the tested M≥4 cells. N, B and S also vary across the matrix.

  2. 02
    FILM is strongest at M=4, with support-type F1 1.000 but high-M undercoverage.

    It leads both M=4 cells (RL 0.192 and 0.133); missed-reference rates rise to 0.875–0.928 in the largest cells.

  3. 03
    V2 preserves high local adjacency, but candidate flooding depresses autonomous set scores.

    Adjacency F1 stays between 0.484 and 0.711, while RL remains 0.028–0.037 because V2 emits many alternatives per reference.

Efficiency. Pooled median attack time across 80 run-level measurements per method: DAGER 13.9s · FILM 53.4s · V2 166.8s. None of the 240 evaluations received nonzero full-exact-set F1.

    Limitations and interpretation

    The evaluated methods are adaptations: DAGER is adapted for Pythia/GPT-NeoX, FILM is a Pythia adaptation with autonomous reordering, and V2 emits autonomous fragments. For DAGER, at exact full row rank the subspace becomes the whole feature space and can no longer discriminate between candidates. One run at this rank wall was marked unsupported and retained as zero rather than applying an unlabeled heuristic truncation; this is a method-level outcome, not an infrastructure failure.

    Ten paired victim runs per cell support uncertainty estimates, but the matrix covers two model sizes and eight prescribed configurations. None of the 240 evaluations received nonzero full-exact-set F1, so partial local or matched reconstruction must not be read as complete sequence recovery.
